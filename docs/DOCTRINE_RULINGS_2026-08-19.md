## A-3 — Tamil month boundary · **CLOSED** rule, **OPEN** at Aavani

> **Amended 2026-08-19 (second pass) [amended].** The first pass ruled this
> CLOSED and called the sunset threshold "the whole rule". That over-claimed in
> two ways: it described the counter-evidence for Aavani as "a single
> unverified almanac reading" when there are several independent sources, and
> it asserted an 8-of-12 disagreement count with no table behind it. The rule
> below stands and the engine is unchanged, but the status is now split: the
> **threshold is settled by the anchor**, the **Aavani boundary is open**, and
> the disagreement count has been **derived and published** rather than
> repeated.

**Ruling.** The sunset threshold is the implemented rule. A sankranti falling
before that day's sunset starts the month on that same civil day; otherwise the
month starts the following day. There are **no per-month exceptions**.

Therefore, for 2026–27 at Chennai:

- Chithirai 1, 2026 = **14 April** (gazetted Puthandu — the anchor)
- Aadi 1, 2026 = **17 July** (both conventions agree; corroborated by the
  gazette's Aadi 27 = 12 August, which implies exactly this)
- Aavani 1, 2026 = **17 August** — **disputed, see below**
- Maasi 1, 2027 = **13 February**

**The disagreement count is now derived.** The sunset and sunrise conventions
differ on **8 of the 12 months** of Tamil year 2026-27 — Chithirai, Vaikasi,
Aani, Aavani, Purattasi, Margazhi, Maasi, Panguni. The full table, with every
sankranti instant, is generated by
[`scripts/derive_tamil_month_boundaries.py`](../scripts/derive_tamil_month_boundaries.py)
into [TAMIL_MONTH_BOUNDARY_TABLE_2026-27.md](TAMIL_MONTH_BOUNDARY_TABLE_2026-27.md),
with a `--check` mode so it cannot silently go stale. The number was right; it
was simply unbacked, which is the same defect this review exists to catch.

**Why the override was still wrong.** The hardcoded `_MONTH_START_DATE_OVERRIDES`
entry forced Aavani 1 to 18 August. That deletion stands — but *not* on the
grounds first given. The honest grounds are narrower: a single date can never
establish a threshold, and a hardcoded per-month patch is the wrong shape of
fix even if 18 August turns out to be the right answer.

### The Aavani conflict — **OPEN**

Multiple independent live panchang sources place Aavani 1, 2026 on **18
August**. That is more than "one unverified reading" and the record should say
so.

**What the second pass established, by computation:** 18 August cannot be
reconciled with the gazetted Puthandu by *any* threshold rule.

| | Sankranti (IST) | Position in daylight | Required assignment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chithirai | 14 Apr 09:32:39 | **0.288** | same day (14 Apr, gazetted) |
| Simha | 17 Aug 07:58:45 | **0.159** | next day (18 Aug, claimed) |

Aavani's crossing falls **earlier** in its day than Chithirai's, yet 18 August
requires it to be deferred while 14 April keeps Chithirai's. No monotone
threshold — sunrise, madhyahna, aparahna, sunset — does both. A 1,001-point
sweep over every such threshold is pinned as an executable test
(`test_no_single_threshold_yields_both_puthandu_and_18_august_aavani`).

So exactly one of these holds, and we do not know which:

1. the 18 August sources compute sankranti by **Vakya** (mean-motion instants
   differing from drik by hours) — the leading hypothesis, and one a drik
   engine structurally cannot chase;
2. they apply the **sunrise** convention, which the gazetted Puthandu excludes;
3. an anchor is misread.

**Note on the gazette anchor.** "Aadi 27 = 12 August" implies Aadi 1 = 17 July,
which is what we produce — genuine corroboration, now pinned by test. It does
**not** independently support 18 August: it constrains where Aadi *starts*, not
how long Aadi runs. Deriving an 18 August Aavani from it requires separately
assuming a 32-day Aadi, and a 32-day Aadi is precisely the sunrise-rule outcome
rather than an independent datum.

**Evidence bar to change the engine:** a named almanac — publisher, edition,
and explicitly whether it is Vakya or Thirukanitham — printing a *month-start
table*. This is now question **Q4** in
[ASTROLOGER_CONSULTATION_2026-08-19.md](ASTROLOGER_CONSULTATION_2026-08-19.md).

**Changed.**

- `app/calculations/tamil_calendar.py` — override dict deleted, along with the
  walk-back branch that existed only to service it. The module docstring now
  records the ruling, the open Aavani conflict, and the bar for reintroducing a
  correction: a named almanac, with publisher, edition, **and whether it is
  Vakya or Thirukanitham**. A Vakya reference cannot be chased with a drik
  engine.
- `app/calculations/tamil_calendar.py` — **split into two steps** on the second
  pass: `find_sankranti_jd` (astronomy) and `month_start_date_for_sankranti`
  (doctrine). Everything the module asserts about Tamil convention now lives in
  one small function, so resolving Aavani cannot require editing a bisection
  search. `find_sankranti_jd` also drops its underscore — `test_panchangam.py`
  was already importing it across a module boundary for Thai Pongal, which made
  the private marking a fiction. The `sun_longitude_at_jd` import is aliased to
  `_sun_sidereal_longitude_at_jd`, because every rasi index here is meaningless
  against a tropical longitude and the bare name does not say which it is.
- `tests/test_tamil_calendar.py` — six tests: the gazetted anchor, Aadi under
  both conventions (plus the gazette's own Aadi 27), Maasi, the **open** Aavani
  boundary marked as such in its docstring, the threshold-impossibility proof,
  and the doctrine step exercised in isolation.
- `scripts/derive_tamil_month_boundaries.py` + `docs/TAMIL_MONTH_BOUNDARY_TABLE_2026-27.md`
  — **new.** Makes the 8-of-12 claim re-derivable, with `--check` so it cannot
  go stale.
- `web/components/dashboard-calendar-shared.tsx` — **a second copy of the same
  override lived here**, in a hardcoded month-start approximation table that the
  frontend uses independently of the backend. Removed.

**Follow-up, not blocking.** That frontend table is a year-independent
approximation of a calculation the backend does properly from the ephemeris. It
is *already* a day off the backend for Karthigai, Thai and Panguni in 2026 —
independent of the override. It should be replaced by the server-supplied Tamil
date. Flagged in a comment at the table; not fixed here because it is a
different problem from A-3.

## A-1 — Kandaka Sani (கண்டக சனி) · **CLOSED (labelled)**

**Ruling.** Kandaka Sani is Saturn in the **4th, 7th or 10th from the Janma
Rasi**, and it is a **layered** name, not a separate axis.

Recorded as `[TAMIL_LINEAGE]` — this is our lineage's practice, not the only
defensible reading. Kandaka is variously reckoned from Lagna, Janma Rasi or
Arudha Lagna elsewhere.

**What this overturns.** We counted from the **Lagna** over the four kendras
(1/4/7/10). The briefing was candid that this was chosen because it *avoided
overlapping* the Moon-reference cycles — a Janma-Rasi Kandaka at the 4th is
already Ardhashtama Sani. That non-overlap was an engineering preference
presented as a modelling virtue. The overlap is the rule.

Two consequences:

1. **The affected population changes almost entirely.** Most people's Lagna and
   Moon sign differ, so Lagna-4/7/10 and Moon-4/7/10 select nearly disjoint
   groups. This is the single widest behavioural change in this pass.
2. **The 1st is no longer Kandaka** — that position is Janma Sani's.

**Layering means naming, not double-scoring.** A reader with Saturn in the 4th
from the Moon is now told *both* "Ardhashtama Sani" and "Kandaka Sani". The
penalty is still applied once: one placement, one score.

**Changed.**

- `app/calculations/transits.py` — `classify_kandaka_cycle` now takes the house
  from the Moon and fires on {4, 7, 10}.
- Six call sites repointed from `saturn_house_from_lagna` to
  `saturn_house_from_moon`: `ask_vinaadi_service`, `daily_guidance_service`,
  `life_areas_service` (×3), `qa_service`.
- `career_service` — **the Kandaka call there was a no-op** and was removed
  rather than repointed. It read
  `saturn_house_from_lagna == 10 and kandaka.is_active`, and under the old
  Lagna-referenced 1/4/7/10 set house 10 *always* implied Kandaka active. Under
  the new reference that conjunction would have silently narrowed who receives
  the career warning — a behaviour change smuggled in under a doctrine fix. The
  10th-from-Lagna rule is a bhava judgement about the career house and stands on
  its own.
- `daily_guidance_service` — the existing "don't double-count" guard is retained
  and re-commented. It was written to dodge an overlap that could not previously
  occur from two different reference points; it is what keeps the penalty single
  now that the overlap is real.
- Labels updated on every surface: `display_names.py`, `life_areas_service.py`,
  `narrative_engine.py`, `web/lib/family-flags.ts`,
  `web/components/dashboard-chart-explanation.tsx` (which had its own
  `classifyKandakaFromLagna` helper keyed off `KENDRA_HOUSES` — now
  `classifyKandakaFromMoon` with its own 4/7/10 set).
- `scripts/generate_rulebook_appendix.py` — the GO-10 table row and its
  surrounding prose, including an explicit note that the overlap is by design.
- Tests updated in `test_calculations.py`, `test_transits_calculations.py`,
  `test_rulebook_invariants.py`, plus a new test asserting the layering directly.

## A-2 — Nethiram (நேத்திரம்) and Jeevan (ஜீவன்) · **OPEN**

**Ruling: do not change anything.**

Two tempting fixes were both rejected:

- Do **not** move the Nethiram cutoff from `d ≤ 2` to `d ≤ 3` to repair the
  2026-08-10 case.
- Do **not** replace the symmetric ring distance with a directional inclusive
  count merely because every other count in this codebase is directional.

One live data point cannot choose between "the threshold is wrong" and "the
distance metric is wrong", and this exact formula was confirmed verbally on
2026-07-16 and then contradicted by a live case in August. Fitting a curve to a

**Evidence bar to close this:** the printed rule or table, **or** roughly 20
independent dated observations from a panchangam that prints Nethiram/Jeevan
daily — enough to fit a rule and *show the fit* rather than assert it.

**Scope reassurance, unchanged:** both markers are display-only. Neither feeds
any score, ranking or recommendation. This is a visible wrong value, not a wrong
recommendation.

## A-4 — Kala Sarpa · **CLOSED** (mechanics) / **CLOSED (labelled)** (naming)

**Ruling on the four mechanical points:**

1. **Seven grahas only** — the Lagna is *not* required to fall inside the arc for
   the yoga to form.
2. **Actual longitude**, not whole-sign placement.
3. **No arbitrary degree tolerance** at the node ends.
4. **A graha exactly on a node is a boundary case**, recorded as a variant rather
   than silently resolved in or out.

**Amended [amended].** The proposed rule that Rahu→Ketu is Kala Sarpa while
Ketu→Rahu is "Kala Amrita" is a recognisable modern-school distinction, but it
is **not** settled Tamil doctrine and must not be baked in as though it were.
The direction nomenclature is therefore recorded as a **declared school
convention**, separately from the enclosure mechanics, which close.

This matters because Kala Sarpa is unusually heavy language for a reader.
Qualification criteria that are too loose tell people they carry a serious
affliction they do not have.

## A-7 — Sthree Deergham (ஸ்திரீ தீர்க்கம்) · **BLOCKED** on the boundary

**Amended [amended].** The proposed answer was a three-state rule with
`≥ 13 = Uttama`. That over-commits. The Tamil wording found in review is
**"13க்கு மேல்"** — *above* 13, not "13 and above" — and a comparative survey of
panchangas gives **7–13 Madhyama, above 13 Uttama**. Count 13 is genuinely
ambiguous between the two readings.

**Ruled:** adopt the three-state architecture —

| Count (inclusive, boy's star from girl's) | Grade |
|---|---|
| 1–6 | இல்லை (fails) |
| 7–13 | மத்திமம் (Madhyama) |
| 14–27 | உத்தமம் (Uttama) |

— with **13 held at the Madhyama end** and flagged as an explicit boundary until
a canonical source settles it. Do not encode `≥ 13 = Uttama` unless the page
says "13 and above".

**Why this is BLOCKED rather than CLOSED.** Moving from binary to three states
is not a threshold edit; it changes the shape of a porutham result across the
backend, the shared client, mobile and web, and it needs one decision that has
not been made: **how a Madhyama result scores and displays**. The briefing
itself raised this for Dinam (§A-6: "a third state, or rounded to pass, or
rounded to fail?") and never resolved it. Under the current lenient rule counts
8–27 pass; under the new bands, whether 7–13 passes decides whether ~19% of
pairings change verdict, and in which direction.

**Not implemented, deliberately.** See "Open questions" at the foot of this
document.

## A-9 — Abhijit Muhurtham (அபிஜித் முகூர்த்தம்) · **CLOSED**

**Ruling.** Abhijit is the **8th of the 15 equal muhurtas that divide the
daylight span**. Its width is therefore `(sunset − sunrise) / 15` and it is
centred on the midpoint of daylight by construction. **Wednesday is the only
weekday exclusion.**

The fixed ±24-minute window was the clock-table simplification. It only ever
coincided with the real width near the equinox at low latitude — which is why
the briefing correctly identified a user in London as the case where it goes
substantially wrong.

**Changed.** `app/calculations/panchangam.py`. Three tests added: the
construction itself, a London summer-vs-winter test (the old rule gave 48
minutes for both; the new one gives ~67 vs ~32), and a test that Wednesday is
the *only* excluded weekday.

**Carried forward, not implemented:** the same source states Abhijit is not
suitable for **marriage** and **Upanayana**. That is an activity-level rule in
the muhurta engine rather than a panchangam window property, and it is listed as
an open question below rather than guessed at.

## A-11 — Tara Bala (தாரா பலம்) · **CLOSED** with a semantic split

**Amended [amended].** "Adverse = {3, 5, 7}, and Janma is not a fourth" is too
flat. Standard panchanga presentations commonly mark **Janma Tara itself as not
good**, alongside Vipat, Pratyari and Naidhana.

**Ruled:** distinguish two concepts rather than collapsing them —

```
CORE_SEVERE_TARAS      = {3, 5, 7}   # Vipat, Pratyak, Naidhana
GENERAL_TARABALA_CAUTION = {1, 3, 5, 7}   # adds Janma
```

The severe set governs scoring. The caution set governs what a reader-facing
Tarabala panel may reasonably mark unfavourable. Activity-specific muhurta rules
then decide how Janma is treated for a given beginning, which is where that
judgement belongs. Janma counting as 1 (not 0) is unchanged and still pinned.

## A-15 — Saturn's BAV table proxied for Rahu and Ketu · **CLOSED**

**Ruling: remove the proxy. The nodes are omitted from bindu-based transit
scoring entirely.**

Rahu and Ketu have no Bhinnashtakavarga table. Substituting Saturn's was
attributed in a code comment to "common Thirukanitham practice" — an attribution
nothing in this repository sourced. Do **not** switch to a different pairing
(Saturn for Rahu, Mars for Ketu) either: the failure was never *which* graha was
borrowed, it was borrowing without a source.

**A second defect fell out of this.** `get_av_bindu` returned a "neutral" 4 for
any graha with no table, and every caller treats `bindus >= 4` as a supportive
transit worth **+8**. The neutral default was therefore silently handing
table-less grahas a bonus. The function now returns `int | None` and callers
skip the adjustment entirely.

**Changed.** `ashtakavarga.py`, `_dg_scoring.py`, `wealth_service.py` (with an
explicit note not to write the None-guard as `or 4`, which would promote a
genuine 0 bindu to 4), `web/components/dashboard-chart-explanation.tsx` (which
mirrored the proxy client-side), and the tests on both sides that pinned it.
`bav_derived.py` always refused the proxy; the two layers now agree.

## A-17 — Murthi inside Ezharai Sani · **CLOSED (labelled)**

├── cycle classification — Ezharai · Ardhashtama · Ashtama · Kandaka

## A-18 — Mahendra count direction · **CLOSED**

**Ruling: girl → boy.** The count runs from the bride's nakshatra to the
groom's, the girl's star being the base and counting as 1. We had it recorded
the other way round.

**No output changes**, and that is exactly why it needed fixing rather than
shrugging at: `{4, 7, 10, 13, 16, 19, 22, 25}` is closed under `c → 29 − c`, and
the two count directions around a 27-star ring always sum to 29 — so the set is
direction-blind and the wrong direction was **invisible**. That symmetry is an
accident of this particular set, not a general property. Any future edit to the
set breaks the accident silently. The existing symmetry test pins it.

# Part 2 — owner decisions

## B-1 — Swiss Ephemeris licensing · **DECIDED — action required**

**Ruling: purchase the Astrodienst Professional Licence.**

Swiss Ephemeris is dual-licensed: AGPL-3.0, or a paid professional licence.
Astrodienst's own licensing page states the choice must be made **before**
software containing it is distributed or a public service is activated, and that
choosing AGPL requires the whole software project to be under AGPL or a
compatible licence.

For a proprietary commercial product with paid tiers, that is the decision. The
precise phrasing matters, though: it is not "AGPL is forbidden", it is **do not
launch under AGPL unless you have deliberately decided to satisfy its
source-licensing obligations** — which for this product would mean offering
complete corresponding source, including the interpretation logic, to every user
of the service.

The mobile build **distributes** the library to devices rather than only serving
results from a server, so it needs its own answer under the same licence.

**Still required, none of which an engineer can do alone:**

1. Purchase the professional licence; file the invoice and terms.
2. Add `LICENSE` / `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES` at the repo root stating the choice and
   the Swiss Ephemeris attribution.
3. Have the signed terms reviewed by someone with commercial-licensing
   authority.

This remains the sharpest item in the review because it is the only one that
gets *harder* after launch. Gated in
[GO_LIVE_CHECKLIST.md](launch/GO_LIVE_CHECKLIST.md) §3a.

# Open questions blocking further work

These are the decisions I could not make from the ruling as given. Each blocks a
specific change that is otherwise ready.

1. **A-7 — how does a Madhyama Sthree Deergham score and display?** A third
   state, rounded to pass, or rounded to fail? Under today's lenient rule counts
   8–27 pass. Whether 7–13 passes decides the verdict for roughly 19% of
   pairings, and in which direction. This also settles the identical question
   the briefing left open for Dinam (A-6).

2. **A-7 — does the canonical Tamil source say "13 and above" or "above 13"?**
   Held at Madhyama pending the page.

3. **A-8 — the p.69 re-check.** One look at the printed page and the Vasya row
   changes.

4. **A-9 — the marriage/Upanayana exclusion.** The source says Abhijit is
   unsuitable for both. Should the muhurta engine exclude Abhijit credit for
   those activity types, or is that a stricter reading than we want to ship?

5. **A-3 — Aavani 1, 2026: 17 or 18 August?** Added on the second pass. The
   engine says 17 August; several live sources say 18. The two cannot both come
   from a threshold rule (proof pinned by test), so the question is really
   *which system* the 18 August sources compute in. Needs a named almanac
   stating **Vakya or Thirukanitham** and printing a month-start table. If the
   answer is Vakya, the fix is a separate Vakya sankranti source — never a
   per-month patch on drik output.
