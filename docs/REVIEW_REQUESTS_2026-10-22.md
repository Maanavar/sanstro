## Q1. Krishna Jayanthi: which reckoning?

**Today:** Sri Jayanthi, the Krishna Ashtami of the solar month **Aavani**.

The other reckoning is Gokulashtami, the Krishna Ashtami of the lunar month **Shravana**. The Tamil
Nadu government holiday follows Gokulashtami, and so does the 2027 almanac. The two agree in most
years, including 2027 (25 Aug). They are a month apart in these years:

| Year | Sri Jayanthi (today) | Gokulashtami |
|---|---|---|
| 2020 | 10 Sep | 12 Aug |
| 2028 | 12 Sep | 13 Aug |
| 2031 | 9 Sep | 10 Aug |
| 2036 | 13 Sep | 15 Aug |
| 2039 | 11 Sep | 12 Aug |
| 2044 | 15 Sep | 16 Aug |

**A second problem:** in some years Aavani holds *two* Krishna Ashtamis (2033: 17 Aug and 16 Sep),
and today both are labelled. If you keep Sri Jayanthi, which of the two counts? Does the Rohini star
decide it?

**We need:** the reckoning, and the tie-break if it is Sri Jayanthi.

## Q2. Which moment of the day names a star vratam?

A star lasts about a day, so it usually begins part-way through one civil day and ends during the
next. Which day is the vratam?

| Vratam | What the 2027 almanac does | Today |
|---|---|---|
| **Karthigai** | the star at **sunset** (13 of 13 days; the star at sunrise matched only 7) | star at sunset |
| **Thiruvonam** | the star at **sunrise** (13 of 13; the star at sunset matched only 8) | star at sunrise |
| Rohini | no data in the almanac | star at sunrise |

When a star holds two sunrises in a row, the almanac lists only the **first** day (Thiruvonam,
12 Sep 2027, not 13 Sep). Today the calendar does the same.

**Conflict to settle:** the same publisher's 2026 calendar puts Thiruvonam a day **earlier** than the
sunrise star in 5 of 13 months (for example 19 Jan 2026, not 20 Jan). So that publisher used a
different moment in 2026 than in 2027.

**We need:** for each vratam, the moment that names the day (sunrise, sunset, or the star that holds
most of the day). Is Karthigai Deepam (12 Dec 2027, star at sunrise) right to be a different rule from
the monthly Karthigai vratam (11 Dec 2027)?

## Q3. Sankatahara Chathurthi at moonrise

**Today:** the day on which Krishna Chaturthi holds at **moonrise**. This matches the almanac 12 of 12
in 2027. The old rule, Chaturthi at sunrise, matched only 5.

Two cases needed a tie-break, and both are copied from the almanac:

- **Chaturthi holds two moonrises** (23 and 24 May 2027): the almanac takes the **later** day (24 May).
- **Chaturthi holds neither moonrise** (it began after moonrise on 23 Feb 2027 and ended before
  moonrise on 24 Feb): the almanac takes the day Chaturthi holds at **sunrise** (24 Feb).

**We need:** confirm both tie-breaks, or give the rule.

## Q4. Ayudha Pooja and Vijayadasami at aparahna

**Today:** Ayudha Pooja and Saraswathi Pooja fall on the day Navami holds the **afternoon (aparahna)**,
and Vijayadasami on the day Dasami holds it. This gives 8 Oct and 9 Oct 2027, as the almanac does,
even though Ashtami is still the tithi at sunrise on 8 Oct. Navarathiri begins on the first sunrise of
the lunar month Ashvina (2027: 1 Oct).

**We need:** confirm aparahna for both, or give the moment.

## Q5. A star that falls twice in one Tamil month

A 31-day Tamil month can hold the same star twice, about 27 days apart:

- **Aadi Pooram 2026:** Pooram on 18 Jul (Aadi 2) **and** 14 Aug (Aadi 29). The 2026 calendar kept the
  **second**.
- **Aadi Krithigai 2028:** Karthigai on 17 Jul **and** 13 Aug.

**Today:** both days are labelled, which is the existing practice for Karthigai Deepam and Maasi Magam.

**We need:** which occurrence counts. Is it the first, the second, the one nearer the full moon or new
moon, or the one in a particular lunar month? Is the answer the same for Thai Poosam, Maasi Magam,
Panguni Uthiram and Vaigasi Visakam?

## Q6. Which day is "the Amavasai" or "the Pournami"?

**Today:** the day on which the tithi holds for most of the day, sunrise to sunrise.

The almanac's monthly list puts the day **one day later** five times in 2027:

- Amavasai on 4 Jul, 30 Sep and 28 Nov (today: 3 Jul, 29 Sep, 27 Nov).
- Pournami on 17 Aug and 14 Nov (today: 16 Aug, 13 Nov).

Yet the same almanac gives **Mahalaya Amavasai as 29 Sep**, not 30 Sep.

**We need:** the moment that names the Amavasai and Pournami day for a fast. Is it different from the
one for tharpanam (Mahalaya)?

## Q7. Karinaal list

**Today:** Karinaal are fixed Tamil dates, and Thai has Karinaal on Thai 1, 2, 3, 11 and 17.

The 2027 almanac lists 15, 16, 17, **24 and 30 Jan** 2027. With Thai 1 on 15 Jan, those last two are
**Thai 10 and 16**, not 11 and 17. The almanac also has no Karinaal on **Karthigai 7** (23 Nov 2027;
your 13 Oct ruling restored this day) or on **Margazhi 11** (26 Dec 2027).

**We need:** the Thai days, and whether Karthigai 7 and Margazhi 11 stay.

## Q8. Sevvai dosham: do two minor relief factors cancel it?

**Today:** the dosham reads "cancelled" if there is one major cancellation, **or** any two minor relief
factors. Jupiter aspecting Mars and Jupiter aspecting the 7th lord often come together, so many charts
read "cancelled" with no major cancellation at all.

**We need:** do two minor factors equal one major? If not, what does cancel it?
