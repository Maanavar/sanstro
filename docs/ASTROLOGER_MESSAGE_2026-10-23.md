### Q1. Krishna Jayanthi: which civil date is printed

The night is now chosen as you ruled (Ashtami at nisheetha, with Rohini as the tie-break). The
question is which **date** to print for that night. In seven years the candidates differ:

| Year | Evening that opens the night | Day Ashtami holds at sunrise | Your example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | 6 Sep | 7 Sep | 6 Sep |
| 2025 | 15 Aug | 16 Aug | 15 Aug |
| 2027 | 24 Aug | 25 Aug | 25 Aug |
| 2029 | 31 Aug | 1 Sep | 1 Sep |
| 2030 | 20 Aug | 21 Aug | 21 Aug |
| 2031 | 9 Aug | 10 Aug | 9 Aug |
| 2032 | 27 Aug | 28 Aug | 27 Aug |

In 2024, 2026, 2028 and 2033 both give the same date. The date of the midnight instant itself
matches only 3 of your 11 examples. The evening date matches 8, and the sunrise day matches 7.
No single rule gives all eleven.

- **Q1a.** Are the eleven dates from one panchangam, or from memory across several years?
- **Q1b.** 2031: Ashtami runs 9 Aug 17:26 to 10 Aug 18:32. Rohini begins only on 11 Aug 15:23, so it
  is at neither midnight. What does your panchangam print for 2031, and why?
- **Q1c.** Does Rohini decide the day at all, or only Ashtami? (Sri Jayanthi in Sri Vaishnava homes
  follows Rohini; the smarta Gokulashtami follows Ashtami at midnight. Which one should the app
  print?)

The calendar prints the evening date today (2026: 4 Sep, the government holiday). We also plan to
show the midnight window on a separate line, so the printed date and the hour of worship are two
answers rather than one.

### Q2. Aadi Krithigai: sunset or sunrise

The monthly Karthigai vratam is judged at sunset (pradosham), as you ruled. For Aadi Krithigai,
sunset matches 4 of your 11 examples and sunrise matches 10. Example: in 2028 Krittika runs 13 Aug
16:38 to 14 Aug 17:51. Sunset gives 13 Aug; sunrise gives 14 Aug, your date.

- **Q2.** Is Aadi Krithigai a sunrise observance, unlike the monthly Karthigai vratam?

### Q3. Aadi Krithigai 2025

(Asked separately from Q2 on purpose.) In 2025 Aadi holds Krittika twice. Your §3 rule (the later

- **Q3.** Is 2025 an exception to §3? If so, what makes it one?

### Q5. Karthigai Deepam 2032

Karthigai 2032 holds Krittika at two sunsets: **17 Nov** and **14 Dec**. §3 picks 14 Dec. Your example
is 17 Nov. The other ten years match.

We checked both days. On 17 Nov, Krittika and Pournami are together at sunrise, in the first day or
two of Karthigai (the Sun enters Vrischikam on the evening of 16 Nov). On 14 Dec, Krittika falls on
Trayodasi, not Pournami.

- **Q5.** Which wins in 2032: Krittika's union with the Pournami (17 Nov), or §3's later Krittika
  (14 Dec)? If the union wins, does the same apply to the festivals in Q4?

### Q6. Maasi Magam 2026

Magam runs 2 Mar 07:51 to 3 Mar 07:31, so it holds only the **3 Mar** sunrise (with Pournami). 2 Mar
is the day Magam fills most of, which §0.3 rules out. 2 Mar is possible only if Maasi Magam is
judged at sunset. The calendar shows 3 Mar.

- **Q6.** Is Maasi Magam a sunrise observance?

### Q7. Ayudha Pooja and Vijayadasami 2045

Navami runs 18 Oct 13:34 to 19 Oct 16:08, so it holds both afternoons (aparahna). Dasami runs
19 Oct 16:08 to 20 Oct 18:48 and holds only the 20th's. "The first of two aparahnas" (§6) gives
Ayudha Pooja **18 Oct** and Vijayadasami **20 Oct**, a day apart. In the other 25 years of 2020–2045
the two are consecutive.

- **Q7.** Is "Ayudha Pooja and Vijayadasami are always consecutive" a rule you hold, or an
  observation? If it is a rule, which gives way in 2045: the tie-break, or Vijayadasami's own
  aparahna?

### Q8. Varalakshmi Vratham when the Pournami is a Friday

- **Q8.** Is the second branch ("or the Pournami itself") right?

### Q9. An Ekadashi that holds no sunrise (new)

Sometimes an Ekadashi begins after one sunrise and ends before the next, so it holds no sunrise at
all. It happens about once a year:

| Ekadashi | Runs | Sunrise before (tithi) | Sunrise after (tithi) | Printed almanac |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Krishna, Jul 2026 | 10 Jul 08:18 to 11 Jul 05:24 | 10 Jul 05:48 (Dashami) | 11 Jul 05:48 (Dwadasi) | 10 Jul |
| Krishna, Jul 2027 | 29 Jul 07:35 to 30 Jul 05:15 | 29 Jul 05:53 (Dashami) | 30 Jul 05:53 (Dwadasi) | 29 Jul |

Today the app names **no** Ekadashi that month. Both printed almanacs we have give the day it falls
in, which is a day with Dashami at sunrise.

- **Q9a.** Is a skipped Ekadashi observed on any day? If so, which: the day it falls in (as printed),
  or Dwadasi?
- **Q9b.** Is the day the almanac prints the same as the fasting day? (For example, in smarta practice
  an Ekadashi touched by Dashami at sunrise is often not fasted.) If they differ, we will show them
  as two answers.

For comparison: you ruled that a skipped Pournami or Amavasai takes the day it falls in (§5). We
now apply the same rule to the monthly Shukla Chathurthi, which brings 2026 back to the almanac's
12. We have **not** applied it to Ekadashi, because the fast makes Ekadashi a different question.
