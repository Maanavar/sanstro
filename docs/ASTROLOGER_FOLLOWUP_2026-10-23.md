## Part A. Festival dates: what we built, please confirm

These are now live in the calendar. If any is wrong, tell us and we will change it.

### A1. Krishna Jayanthi: Rohini decides when it meets Ashtami

You answered that only Ashtami decides the smarta Gokulashtami (Q1c). But all
eleven of your example dates follow one rule in which Rohini *does* decide:

> When Krishna Ashtami and Rohini overlap (the Jayanthi yoga), the night whose
> midnight falls inside the overlap; if no midnight does, the day the overlap
> falls on. When they do not overlap, the night with Ashtami at midnight,
> printed on the evening that opens it.

For example, 2027: Ashtami runs 24 Aug 20:24 to 25 Aug 19:19, so it holds only
the midnight of 24 Aug. But Ashtami meets Rohini on 25 Aug, 12:27 to 19:19.
Your example says 25 Aug, and so does Drik Panchang. The same happens in 2029
(1 Sep) and 2030 (21 Aug). Drik Panchang's Chennai dates agree with this rule
in every year it publishes (2021, 2026 to 2031).

- **A1a.** Is this the rule your panchangam follows?
- **A1b.** 2019 is the one disagreement we found. Ashtami and Rohini overlapped
  only on the morning of 24 Aug (03:47 to 08:32). The rule gives **24 Aug**;
  the Tamil Nadu government holiday was **23 Aug**. What did your panchangam
  print for 2019?
- **A1c.** Should the app also show Sri Jayanthi (the Sri Vaishnava day) as a
  separate line? If so, what is its rule?

### A2. Aadi Krithigai: sunrise, and a full day beats a partial one

As you answered (Q2), Aadi Krithigai is now judged at sunrise. Your "first
Krittika" rule (Q3) did not fit the dates, though: it gives 18 Jul for 2028
(your date 14 Aug) and 16 Jul for 2020 (observed 12 Aug). This rule fits all
eleven of your dates and the observed ones:

> If Aadi holds Krittika twice, a day that holds Krittika at sunrise beats one
> where Krittika begins and ends between two sunrises. If both hold a sunrise,
> the one in the lunar month that begins inside Aadi.

In 2025 the second Krittika (16 Aug) held no sunrise, so 20 Jul wins: your
date, and AstroVed's.

- **A2a.** Is this right?
- **A2b.** 2019: Krittika ran from 26 Jul 18:56 to 27 Jul 19:30. The app says
  **27 Jul**. The Kapaleeswarar temple, Mylapore, held its Aadi Krithigai
  procession on the evening of **26 Jul**, the evening the star began. Is that
  a temple custom (worship on the evening the star arrives), or should the
  app's date be 26 Jul?

### A3. Karthigai Deepam and the full moon

As you answered (Q5), when the Karthigai month holds Krittika twice, the one
beside the month's full moon wins: 2032 is **17 Nov**. The same rule gives
17 Nov 2013, the day Tiruvannamalai lit the Maha Deepam. When the full-moon
test cannot decide (Vaigasi Visakam 2022 and 2041), the later lunar month does
(12 Jun), as you answered for Q4.

- **A3.** Please confirm.

### A4. Ayudha Pooja when Navami covers two afternoons

As you answered (Q7), Ayudha Pooja takes the later of the two afternoons, the
one next to Dasami. The Tamil Nadu government order for 2018 agrees: Navami
covered the afternoons of 17 and 18 Oct, and Ayudha Pooja was **18 Oct**. 2045
is now **19 Oct**, the day before Vijayadasami (20 Oct).

- **A4.** Please confirm.

### A5. Varalakshmi Vratham: we kept the Pournami-Friday rule

You answered (Q8) that the vratham is always the Friday *before* the Shravana
Pournami, never the Pournami itself, and that 2022 was observed on 5 Aug. We
could not find any source for 5 Aug 2022. Drik Panchang (Chennai) gives
**12 Aug 2022**, **28 Aug 2026** and **24 Aug 2029**, the Pournami itself in
all three years when it is a Friday. So we kept the rule from your 22 October
ruling: the last Friday before the Pournami, or the Pournami itself if it is a
Friday. Some Telugu and Tamil calendars print 21 Aug 2026.

- **A5a.** What does your 2022 panchangam print for Varalakshmi Vratham? A
  photo of that page would settle this.
- **A5b.** If your tradition is the strict "Friday before", should the app
  show 21 Aug 2026 as an alternative line?

### A6. An Ekadashi that holds no sunrise

The app now names such an Ekadashi on the day it falls in, the day with
Dashami at sunrise (10 Jul 2026, 29 Jul 2027). That is the day both printed
almanacs give, and Drik Panchang lists it as the smarta fast. Drik lists the
Dwadasi after it (11 Jul 2026) as the Vaishnava and "Gauna" fast. You answered
that the fast is on Dwadasi for everyone (Q9a), which is the Vaishnava day.

- **A6a.** For smarta households in Tamil Nadu, is the fast on the day the
  Ekadashi falls in (as Drik says), or on Dwadasi (as you said)?
- **A6b.** Should the app show the Vaishnava day as a second line?
- **A6c.** Should the app show the parana (fast-breaking) time? Drik gives
  11 Jul 2026, 13:31 to 16:06, after Hari Vasara ends at 10:32. Do you follow
  Hari Vasara?
