# Single-Feature Testing — One Feature Per Persona (P01–P10)

**Method:** For each of the first ten personas, take the **one feature** that sits at the centre of *their* reason for being here — not every widget, just the one that matters to them — and test it against six fixed questions:

1. **What they expected**
2. **What happened**
3. **Whether they understood it**
4. **Whether they trust it**
5. **Whether they'd return tomorrow**
6. **Whether they'd pay for this feature**

Grounded in the real Nova dashboard and in the P01–P10 first-run think-alouds. Answers are in-character and deliberately honest — several are "no" or "partial." No redesign, no fixes; this is a read on the human, not a proposal.

## P01 — Murugesan "Muru" K. · 34 · auto driver, Madurai · low–medium digital · Tamil
**Feature tested: the panchangam strip — Rahu Kalam (red) / Nalla Neram (green)**

- **What they expected:** To find out which hours today are bad to start a long trip — the one astrological thing he already lives by. He came for a caution-window, not a horoscope.
- **What happened:** The strip showed Rahu Kalam in red with the exact time and Nalla Neram in green, right at the top. He found it in seconds and immediately mapped it to real life: "no big airport trip in that window."
- **Understood it? Partial.** The Rahu Kalam / Nalla Neram half he understood instantly and completely — it's his native vocabulary. The *score dial* above it he did **not** understand ("is amber good or bad? I wish it said நல்ல நாள் or ஜாக்கிரதை"). So: the feature he cares about, yes; the number bolted above it, no.
- **Trust it? Yes — for this part.** It matches what the temple almanac tells him, so it lands as "real," the same category as the priest's panchangam. (His trust in the *chart* underneath is shaken by the lat/long numbers he wasn't sure he entered right — but the Rahu Kalam strip itself he trusts.)
- **Return tomorrow? Yes.** This is the single stickiest thing for him: a glanceable, Tamil, morning check before a big-fare day. Rahu Kalam alone is a daily-open habit.
- **Pay for it? No.** He treats Rahu Kalam as free public knowledge — the temple gives it away. He actively fled the Reports tab the moment he saw prices. He'll use this daily and never pay a rupee for it.

## P02 — Aarthi Selvaraj · 28 · engineer, Chennai · Traditional mode · skeptic
**Feature tested: Porutham — the 10-factor match breakdown (Rajju/Vedhai hard-fail gates)**

- **What they expected:** To reverse-engineer *why* her parents keep saying a match "failed on Rajju" — she wanted the receipts behind the verdict, expecting either a black box or a con.
- **What happened:** It laid out all ten poruthams (Dina, Gana, Mahendra, Stree-Dirgha, Yoni, Rasi, Rasiyathipathi, Vasya, Rajju, Vedhai) and explicitly flagged Rajju and Vedhai as hard-fail gates. She could see the exact rule her family applies. (She couldn't fully *run* it — no second person's chart on hand — but the structure answered her real question.)
- **Understood it? Yes, completely.** She read it as a rules engine and immediately grasped the gate logic: "if Rajju fails it's a hard no regardless of the other nine." That's precisely what she came to decode.
- **Trust it? No — and that's fine by her.** She doesn't believe the astrology is *true*; she trusts that the app **faithfully implements the traditional method** her parents use. Two separate judgments: the belief stays rejected, the implementation earns a nod.
- **Return tomorrow? No.** She got what she came for in one sitting — understanding the machine that runs her life. She's a dissect-it-and-uninstall user; there's no daily hook for a skeptic who's resolved her question.
- **Pay for it? No.** She explicitly won't pay to test a hypothesis she's already settled. The value to her was informational and one-time.

- **Return tomorrow? Yes — on demand.** Not a daily ritual, but a high-value on-call tool: he'll open it whenever a signing or launch date comes up, at any hour.

## What the ten feature-tests say, in one breath

- **Highest-trust, highest-intent feature is not the horoscope — it's timing.** Muhurta/Muhurtham Naal (P03) and Porutham (P02, P04) are the features people came *for*, understood, and trusted (as method, if not as belief). The daily score dial, by contrast, confused every low-digital-literacy user (P01, P07, P09) even while the panchangam strip beside it landed cleanly.
- **"Trust" splits from "belief" and from "will pay."** Skeptics (P02, P10) trust the *implementation* while rejecting the astrology, and still extract real value. Devout low-income users (P01, P07) trust it fully and will *never* pay — panchangam reads as free public knowledge.
- **The one persona the app fails is P09** — the non-reading, voice-first fisherman. Every feature is text; his one yes/no question drowns in a paragraph. The gap is a delivery-modality gap (Tamil voice + symbols + verdict), not a content gap.
- **Willingness to pay concentrates in exactly two personas** — P03 (time-poor, cash-fine trader) and, conditionally, P08 (serious hobbyist, for reports). Both pay for *artefacts and on-demand timing*, not for the daily glance. That's the shape of the pay-per-use store's real market.

*End — one feature per persona, tested against the six questions. Behaviour and judgement only; no redesign proposed.*
